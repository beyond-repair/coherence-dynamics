# Mathematical Foundations

## Effective Action

The schematic starting point is the path integral for the effective action after integrating out unresolved degrees of freedom:

\[
e^{i\Gamma_\mu[\phi,g]} = \int \mathcal{D}\chi\, e^{iS[\phi,\chi,g]}.
\]

Specify for each model:

1. Fields and physical meaning.
2. Action and symmetries.
3. Metric signature and unit conventions.
4. Degrees of freedom being integrated out.
5. Regulator and renormalization prescription (where applicable).
6. Boundary and initial conditions.
7. Approximation scheme and domain of validity.
8. Resulting effective action or response kernel.

## Nonlocal Kernels

If the result contains a nonlocal kernel, establish its causal interpretation and determine whether the equations admit stable solutions.

## Additional Stress-Energy

If the effective description contains additional stress-energy, derive it by variation of the action and verify the appropriate conservation identities.

Do not interpret mathematical information loss as physical energy loss without a physical mechanism.

## Spectral Laboratory

Finite-graph and Sierpiński-gasket tools serve as controlled mathematical laboratories.

- Graph Laplacian construction.
- Eigenvalue and eigenvector calculations.
- Boundary-condition comparisons.
- Heat-trace and spectral-zeta calculations where well-defined.
- Convergence across graph levels.
- Continuum-limit investigations where supported.
- Sensitivity to geometry and normalization.

For the operator \(K = \omega^2 I - W L\):

A positivity bound (e.g., \(W < 1/\lambda_{\max}\)) is not a physical prediction of a unique value.

Do not interpret graph eigenvalues as physical couplings unless a derivation establishes the mapping.
